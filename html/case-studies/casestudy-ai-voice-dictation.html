The challenge

Product data everywhere. Business insight nowhere.

The team had product events, ad spend, CRM data, and lifecycle activity living in completely separate systems. None of it connected, and none of it could answer the question leadership actually needed to ask: are users activating, staying, and growing?

01
No unified view of the customer journey

Product events in PostHog, ad conversions in a separate ad platform, support activity in Pylon, and lifecycle campaigns in Customer.io — each team had their own slice of the customer, and no one had the full picture from acquisition through retention.

02
Revenue metrics built in spreadsheets

MRR, ARR, and activation rates had no automated source. Someone had to pull exports, reconcile numbers, and paste them into a spreadsheet before every board or leadership meeting — a process that was slow, error-prone, and always slightly out of date.

03
Growth and retention flying blind

Without a single warehouse, there was no reliable way to compute activation cohorts, feature adoption curves, or retention by segment. The team couldn't tie ad spend to activated users or understand which features kept users coming back.

Data sources connected

13+ systems. One warehouse.

Every system the business runs on — product events, seven ad platforms, link analytics, billing, CRM, and lifecycle — connected, normalized, and loaded into ClickHouse via ClickPipe, dlt, and Fivetran on automated schedules.

What we built

A complete, automated analytics platform.

The full Vero playbook — ingestion, transformation, reporting, and activation — adapted to a product-led SaaS with a ClickHouse warehouse at its core.

01
Unified 13+ sources into ClickHouse via ClickPipe, dlt, and Fivetran

PostHog, RevenueCat, and Customer.io stream in real-time via ClickPipe. Pylon, Vibe, and Dub load via dlt with incremental cursors and state management. Stripe, Facebook Ads, Google Ads, AppLovin, Apple Ads, AppsFlyer, Attio, and Partnerships run on Fivetran managed connectors. 02
Modeled product, growth, and revenue in dbt

A single dbt project — staging → marts — turns raw events and CRM data into trusted definitions the whole company shares. Activation is one definition. Retention cohorts are one definition. MRR and ARR roll up from the same mart that powers every dashboard. No more reconciling numbers before a meeting.

dbt activation model retention cohorts MRR / ARR mart
03
Delivered dashboards in Hex and pushed segments back to Customer.io

Modeled data is served in Hex across three dashboard surfaces: an executive dashboard (MRR, ARR, revenue growth), a product analytics dashboard (activation, feature adoption, retention), and a growth dashboard (ad spend, CAC, funnel). Warehouse-modeled user segments are pushed back into Customer.io via reverse ETL — so lifecycle campaigns fire on real behavior, not guesswork.

How it unfolded

The signals were always there. Nobody could read them.

01 · Signal
Downloads ≠ use
The numbers looked healthy. The engagement picture was hidden.

Downloads were growing across iOS, Mac, Windows, and Android. But each platform reported separately. Nobody could see which users were genuinely engaged, which ones had gone dormant after day one, or whether the growth in downloads was translating into users who actually stayed.

02 · Root Cause
No cross-platform view
Product events, billing, and ad spend had no shared identity.

Without stitching user identity across platforms and billing, engagement dips were invisible. The team couldn't flag users going quiet before they churned. They couldn't see whether a paid channel was producing activated users or just downloads. Spend decisions were based on surface metrics, not retention signals.

03 · Outcome
Engagement visible
At-risk users flagged. Channels ranked by what actually works.

Now the team tracks engagement depth — not just whether someone downloaded, but whether they're actively using the product. At-risk users are surfaced automatically when usage drops below a threshold. Acquisition channels are ranked by real activation rate, so growth spend goes to sources that produce retained users, not just installs.
