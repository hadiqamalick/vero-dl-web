The challenge

Usage-based costs. No idea what a call actually costs.

Synthflow's business runs on per-minute AI infrastructure billed by a dozen vendors, plus product, support, and GTM tools — none of it connected, and split across two regions that couldn't be compared.

01
A dozen usage-billed vendors, zero unit economics

Speech-to-text, text-to-speech, LLM tokens, telephony, and real-time media were each billed in their own portal. Computing the true cost — and margin — of a single voice minute meant stitching together CSVs by hand.

02
Two regions that legally couldn't be combined

US and EU each ran their own MongoDB, chat, and agent databases, and data-residency rules meant raw records couldn't simply be shipped to one place. Any company-wide number meant reconciling two isolated regions by hand.

03
Product, support, and sales in silos

Amplitude, Pylon, Linear, HubSpot, and Gong each held a piece of the customer story. Connecting product usage to a support ticket to a deal was impossible without a person in the middle.

Data sources connected

20+ systems. One warehouse.

Every system Synthflow runs on — the AI/voice infrastructure, the cloud bill, the application databases, and the product, support, and GTM tools — connected, normalized, and loaded on schedule into BigQuery.

What we built

A complete, automated analytics platform.

The same repeatable Vero playbook, adapted to Synthflow's usage-based economics and dual-region setup — running on their own infrastructure, fully owned by their team.

01
Tokenized at the edge, centralized safely

A dedicated Dagster deployment runs inside each region — US and EU — extracting from that region's MongoDB, chat, and agent databases and tokenizing sensitive fields before anything leaves. Only tokenized data moves to the central legacy cluster, so data-residency rules hold while the company still gets one unified view.

Dagster · US & EU in-region tokenization data residency
02
Unified 20+ sources and modeled true COGS

The central legacy cluster pulls the tokenized regional data alongside 20+ usage, cost, and GTM sources via dlt, with schema evolution and row-count asset checks on every load. One dbt project — staging → intermediate → mart — turns it all into real cost-per-minute, gross margin, and a single global model the whole company trusts.

dlt dbt global model
03
Automated end to end — into Hex and back out

Sensor-driven orchestration ties it together: a successful load triggers the matching dbt models, and a migration sensor kicks off the global model once both regions land. Modeled data is served in Hex and pushed back into Pylon, Linear, and Rocketlane via reverse ETL — and every failure posts straight to Slack.
