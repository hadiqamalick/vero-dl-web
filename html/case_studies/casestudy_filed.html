The challenge

Two journeys. Neither one visible.

A prospect's path from first web visit to paying workspace, and a trial user's path from signup to product engagement, both invisible. Each lived in a separate system with no way to connect them.

01
Trial users going quiet with no warning

Workspaces signed up for trials, received credits, and started tax preps, but account managers had no way to see who was completing returns vs. abandoning them halfway. By the time a trial went cold, the window to intervene had already closed.

02
The deal journey had no single thread

Ad spend lived in three separate portals. Leads were in HubSpot. Product usage was in Postgres. There was no way to trace a prospect from first web session through MQL, meeting, and trial to a paying workspace. Attribution was a spreadsheet exercise done after the fact.

03
Product deploys silently broke reports

Postgres schemas changed with every product release. Columns disappeared, names shifted, no alerts, no contracts. Reports went stale between deploys and stakeholders saw wrong numbers without knowing. Nobody trusted the data.

What we built

A complete, automated analytics platform.

The same repeatable Vero playbook, adapted to Filed's full-funnel stack, running on Azure, fully owned by their team.

01
Connected all 12 sources into a central warehouse

Marketing channels run on Fivetran for low-maintenance, managed ingestion. Product databases and HubSpot run on dlt pipelines scheduled via Dagster, with schema evolution built in, so new Postgres columns no longer break downstream reports. PostHog and Sequence connect directly. Everything lands in Snowflake on schedule.

How it unfolded

Two journeys. Both finally visible.

The deal journey and the user journey were running in parallel, and both were invisible. Once the data was connected, the team could see every step of both.

01 · Signal
Trials going dark
Workspaces signed up. Nobody knew what happened next.

Trial workspaces received credits and started tax preps, but account managers had no performance view. Without joining Postgres product data (completed vs. incomplete returns) to HubSpot deal stages, there was no way to know which trials were engaging and which had quietly gone dormant.

02 · Root Cause
No shared identity
Three ad portals. One CRM. One product DB. No thread between them.

Google, Facebook, and LinkedIn spend lived in separate portals. Leads were in HubSpot. Product usage was in Postgres. Schemas changed with every deploy and broke reports silently. There was no single model that could answer "where did this trial come from and are they likely to convert?"

03 · Outcome
Both journeys live
Trial health per workspace. Full funnel from first click to paid.

Account managers now see a performance indicator per trial workspace, completed returns vs. incomplete, credit usage, active seats. The Lead-to-Cash model traces every prospect from first web session through MQL and meeting to active workspace. And when adspend per MQL climbs above threshold, the alert fires before the team notices.
